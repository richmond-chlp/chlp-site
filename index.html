The health effects of climate programs, for each dollar spent

The Climate Health Ledger Project (CHLP) builds an open tool that lets implementers and funders estimate how much a climate program improves people's health for each dollar spent. CHLP will check shared results against the implementer's own records: every result until 20 have been shared, then a random sample. How the checks work

Both tools are free, in open beta, and run in your browser. Nothing you type leaves the page unless you send it.

Why it matters

Climate options are commonly ranked by what it costs to cut one metric ton of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e), and those rankings leave health out.1

Health also receives little of the money. When a decision leaves health out, it counts health as zero.

4.9%

At most, the share of bilateral and multilateral adaptation finance committed to health, 2009 to 2019.2

How CHLP measures

CHLP uses units that funders and governments already use: disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) for health, and the net change in metric tons of CO2e for climate. Animal welfare, where it applies, is measured separately.

Each assessment compares a program with published benchmarks for programs of the same kind. Each is reported in its own units, with an optional combined score beside them, never in their place. A program whose estimated net effect on health is a loss beyond a margin set in advance is not recommended, whatever other measures show.

The tools

For implementers

Program Estimator

Enter what a program delivers and what it costs, as a low, central and high figure where possible. The estimator applies published, dated factors from CHLP's desk assessments. It returns a cost per DALY averted, with a range, and the net change in emissions, counting only what the money added. It takes about 30 minutes.

Who does what

Implementers

Run climate programs. They enter what a program delivers and what it costs, in units they already record, and use the tool free.

Funders and agencies

Read each result a program shares, with its inputs, sources and factors, and can offer the tool to the programs they fund.

CHLP

Publishes the factors behind the tool, will check shared results against implementers' records, and will evaluate in the field where the estimates matter most.

How the checks work

CHLP checks only results that implementers choose to share, with their consent. Every shared result is checked until 20 have been shared. After that, results are drawn at random within each intervention, by a rule registered in advance, and neither CHLP nor a funder picks or excludes one.

Each check compares the entry with the implementer's own records on four points:

  • the units delivered;
  • the spending;
  • the basis for the share that would have happened anyway;
  • whether the program and its setting fit the factors the estimate used.

CHLP then re-runs the arithmetic. A check ends as confirmed (inside the entry's range), revised (outside it) or not checkable (records missing), and the entry's label then says what was checked and when. The implementer sees the check 30 days before it is recorded, can correct factual errors, and can publish a response alongside. Where checks show that a factor is off, the tool's next version revises it.

A check tests what was delivered and spent; only a field evaluation tests the health effect of each unit. CHLP chooses where to evaluate in the field by where the tool's estimates matter most. A cofounder or adviser who is an epidemiologist would lead each field evaluation.

Independence

  • CHLP discloses every funder, and no funder steers what it evaluates or blocks what it publishes. Fees do not depend on the result, and the questions are registered before the work begins.
  • CHLP publishes every result. Whoever is evaluated sees the findings 30 days before publication, can correct factual errors, and can publish a response alongside.
  • CHLP never evaluates a program it runs, does not review the grants of a funder that gives it general support, and discloses any funding link between its backers and a program it evaluates.
