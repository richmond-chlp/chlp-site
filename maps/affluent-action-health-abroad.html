What the map shows

  • The map leaves heat aside. The climate ledger's bridge from emissions to health already counts the effects of temperature, most of which fall in poorer countries. These rows cover the other routes.
  • The largest measured harms come through supply chains and land: smoke from coal-fired nickel smelting in Indonesia, and haze from land clearing. Neither has been divided by the share of demand that rich-country climate policy causes.
  • The largest gains come through cheaper technology and cleaner fuels: solar in place of kerosene, and cleaner stoves. Each carries a doubt of its own, lead from discarded solar batteries and cookstove credits that overstate the carbon saved.
  • Four routes run through prices and budgets rather than pollution: food prices, gas supply, border charges and aid. Only food prices has a measured link to child health, through wasting. The other three rest on general relations between income or power supply and deaths.
  • Eleven assessments, N85 to N95, now price one route each, and none reaches the reference point of $100 per DALY. The nearest are pollution controls at Indonesia's nickel smelters (N85, $4,100 per DALY) and spot gas to keep Pakistan's power on (N90, $5,730), both after the adjustment. Three harms price as break-even values instead: food prices (N89), the border charge (N92) and aid moved to climate (N95).
  • Under CHLP's rule, each ledger keeps its own line. A gain abroad does not offset a harm at home, or the reverse.

  • How the signs were set. A sign follows the cited study. The climate sign is a benefit by definition for a climate measure, and mixed where the cited source shows a cost to the climate as well. A route with a plausible mechanism and no health measure carries a question mark. Each intervention’s panel names the source behind each sign, with a link where one exists, and says so where a sign is a judgment. A source read only in part, or only as an abstract or a summary, is marked.
  • How main and minor were set. By judgment. A published figure matched 3 of the links to a market or to finance.
  • What the map leaves out. Heat and every other temperature effect, which the climate ledger counts. Any weighing of a gain abroad against a cost at home. Effects of these actions on health in the affluent countries themselves, which maps 1 and 4 cover.
