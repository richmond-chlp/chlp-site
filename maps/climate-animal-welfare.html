How to read a map

The $100 reference point
CHLP sets every assessment against $100 per DALY averted, the figure in Coefficient Giving's request for Launchpad Funds. Other funders use other bars, so each result also appears as a multiple: at 50 times the reference point, a DALY costs $5,000.
The counterfactual adjustment
Money spent on something that would happen anyway buys nothing. Each assessment divides its cost by one minus the chance that the purchase would happen without the funder, so a 50% chance doubles the cost. Figures appear before and after it.
DALYs and QALYs
A DALY is a year of healthy life lost to illness, disability or early death, and the assessments count DALYs averted. A QALY is a year of life weighted by its quality; CHLP takes one DALY averted as equal to one QALY gained.
National bars
Many assessments also compare their cost with two national bars per QALY: the value a government sets, and the cost at which its health spending buys a QALY. The second comes from Pichon-Riviere and others' estimates for 174 countries; where one study measured both units, DALYs ran from 81% to 135% of QALYs.
A desk assessment
A desk assessment estimates the cost per DALY from published studies and public figures. CHLP has not evaluated these programs in the field, and the bodies that run them did not review the assessments.
The Pathfinder review
The Lancet Pathfinder Commission reviewed studies of climate actions and their effects on health in 2023. The maps cite its 200 actions, and the health effects of most of them are modeled rather than measured.
Prices, keys and terms
Figures are in US dollars at September 2026 prices, with later years discounted at 3%. Each assessment has a key, such as Key N23, for citing and searching; LMIC means low- and middle-income countries.

What the map shows

  • The two ledgers conflict most often through efficiency. Chicken for beef, higher output from each animal, and housing with less space all lower emissions for each unit of food and worsen or multiply animal lives.
  • The conflict runs both ways. Slower-growing chickens and free-range hens are gains for the animals and raise emissions in the studies read. The rise is 6% to 30% for chicken and up to 60% for a full shift in eggs.
  • Carbon markets pay for a ton. None of the sources read reports a credit method with a term for the animals affected, and the methods themselves were not checked for one.
  • Plant-rich diets are the one large shift in which both ledgers gain. They have no market, and the programs that promote them have not, on average, changed what people eat.
  • The number of animals is the term that carbon accounting misses. Fish, shrimp and insects could carry most of the growth in farmed animals to 2033, and each is promoted as low-carbon protein. The counts are rough, and one insect estimate spans a factor of fifty.
  • Wild animals enter through energy and land. The harm at turbines and dams is measured and has known remedies. For fire, the severity of a burn matters more than who lit it.
  • Every row has a CHLP assessment, and two hold a conflict within one assessment: the livestock ammonia assessment (N13) is a gain for birds and likely warming, and the sow farm air filtration assessment (N20) helps pigs and cuts emissions only if output holds. Under CHLP's rule, each ledger keeps its own line.

  • How the signs were set. A sign follows a CHLP assessment where one exists and the cited study elsewhere. The climate sign is a benefit by definition for a climate measure and follows the cited study for a welfare reform. Several welfare figures come from advocacy research groups, which are named where used. Whether more animals alive is a gain or a loss depends on how those animals live; the map counts a larger number of farmed animals as a harm and says so here. Each intervention’s panel names the source behind each sign, with a link where one exists, and says so where a sign is a judgment. A source read only in part, or only as an abstract or a summary, is marked.
  • How main and minor were set. By judgment, as on maps 1 and 2. A published figure matched 12 of the links to a market or to finance.
  • What the map leaves out. Any weighing of one ledger against the other. The size of most welfare effects. N37 and N38 count the time birds spend in pain, by pain level, for each dollar; the other rows give a sign only. Human health, on maps 1 and 2, and mental health, on map 4.
