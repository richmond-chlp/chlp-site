How to read a map

The $100 reference point
CHLP sets every assessment against $100 per DALY averted, the figure in Coefficient Giving's request for Launchpad Funds. Other funders use other bars, so each result also appears as a multiple: at 50 times the reference point, a DALY costs $5,000.
The counterfactual adjustment
Money spent on something that would happen anyway buys nothing. Each assessment divides its cost by one minus the chance that the purchase would happen without the funder, so a 50% chance doubles the cost. Figures appear before and after it.
DALYs and QALYs
A DALY is a year of healthy life lost to illness, disability or early death, and the assessments count DALYs averted. A QALY is a year of life weighted by its quality; CHLP takes one DALY averted as equal to one QALY gained.
National bars
Many assessments also compare their cost with two national bars per QALY: the value a government sets, and the cost at which its health spending buys a QALY. The second comes from Pichon-Riviere and others' estimates for 174 countries; where one study measured both units, DALYs ran from 81% to 135% of QALYs.
A desk assessment
A desk assessment estimates the cost per DALY from published studies and public figures. CHLP has not evaluated these programs in the field, and the bodies that run them did not review the assessments.
The Pathfinder review
The Lancet Pathfinder Commission reviewed studies of climate actions and their effects on health in 2023. The maps cite its 200 actions, and the health effects of most of them are modeled rather than measured.
Prices, keys and terms
Figures are in US dollars at September 2026 prices, with later years discounted at 3%. Each assessment has a key, such as Key N23, for citing and searching; LMIC means low- and middle-income countries.

What the map shows

  • Rules, bans and corporate pledges drive most of these interventions. One traded unit was found, the cage-free credit, and it pays for housing with no health term.
  • Infection carries the strongest measured evidence. Limits on farm antibiotics, the closure of live poultry markets and the vaccination of dogs each have a measured or closely observed effect in people.
  • Several welfare reforms carry a human cost on the evidence read. Workers breathe more dust in aviaries, pork costs more under a crate ban, and a ban on wild meat removes protein. Only the pork price is measured as an outcome.
  • Most signs are unmeasured. Claims that welfare reform lowers pandemic risk rest on mechanism for cage-free housing and transport. Mink farming is the exception, with documented passage of a virus to people.
  • The money is small beside farm support. Tracked advocacy spending is about $260 million a year and alternative protein investment $881 million, against about $842 billion a year in public transfers to agriculture.
  • Health is absent from the pledges and from the credit. No pledge or rule read sets a health term. Among the sources read, only the hen housing trial and one barn dust trial measured workers.
  • Every one of the 18 rows has a CHLP assessment: N37 to N52, with N13 and N20. Under CHLP's rule, animal welfare stays on its own line and enters no combined figure with health.

  • Read end to end, November 21, 2026. Yu and others, 2014, on live market closure. Fernandez-Rodriguez and others, 2025, the pooled analysis of plant-based meat trials. Slade and Alleyne, 2021, on slaughterhouse work. Karlsson and others, 2025. The Wageningen report of 2025, with its appendices. Reference lists and online supplements were not read. By search for the relevant passages only: the Norwegian monitoring report for 2024, 178 pages, and the OECD report on farm support, 718 pages.
  • Read as an abstract only, November 26, 2026: Senthilselvan and others, 1997, American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine, on dust suppression with canola oil in swine barns.
  • How the signs were set. A sign follows a CHLP assessment where one exists and the cited study elsewhere. A sign marked unmeasured is a mechanism with no human outcome in what was read. Several sources come from parties with a stake. Advocacy groups supply the pledge counts, and an industry council one salmon figure. A pork industry body paid for the review of farm neighbors, an egg industry coalition for the hen housing trial, and a product maker for the diet trial. Each intervention’s panel names the source behind each sign, with a link where one exists, and says so where a sign is a judgment. A source read only in part, or only as an abstract or a summary, is marked.
  • How main and minor were set. By judgment. Philanthropy is marked as a main source where campaigns it pays for are the reason a pledge or rule exists; the money itself seldom reaches a farm. A published figure matched 6 of the links to a source of money.
  • What the map leaves out. The effect on the animals for most rows, which each row assumes is a gain and which some reforms dispute. N37, N38 and N49 now count the time animals spend in pain, by pain level, for each dollar. The size of each health effect. Climate, which map 3 traces, and mental health, which map 4 traces.
